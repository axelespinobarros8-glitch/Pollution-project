HOW DOES AIR POLLUTION EFFECT THE ATMOSPHERE?

Air pollution damages the atmosphere and human well-being by reducing air quality, creating toxic smog, and trapping heat. Key consequences include respiratory harm, acid rain, and climate change .

Human Health Impacts

  • Lung damage: Tiny particles and gases inflame airways, causing asthma attacks and lower lung function.
  • Systemic disease: Long-term exposure increases risks for heart attacks, strokes, and lung cancer.

Environmental Impacts

  • Acid rain: Sulfur and nitrogen gases mix with rain clouds, destroying plant leaves, degrading soils, and eroding stone buildings.
  • Ground-level ozone: Chemical reactions in sunlight form toxic smog that hurts plant growth and reduces crop yields

Human Activities

  • Transportation: Cars, trucks, trains, and planes release exhaust, carbon monoxide, and nitrogen oxides from burning gasoline and diesel.
  • Industry and Power Plants: Factories and electricity generation stations burn coal, oil, and natural gas, emitting sulfur dioxide and thick smoke.
  • Agriculture: Farming practices use chemicals and fertilizers, while livestock production releases methane gas into the air.
  • Household Combustion: Burning wood, coal, or kerosene for indoor cooking and heating creates dangerous smoke and particulate matter.

Natural Sources

  • Wildfires: Uncontrolled forest and field fires release massive amounts of smoke, ash, and greenhouse gases.
  • Volcanic Eruptions: Volcanoes spew sulfur gases, ash, and rock particles directly into the upper atmosphere.
  • Windblown Dust: Strong winds pick up dust, dirt, and pollen from deserts and dry soil.
